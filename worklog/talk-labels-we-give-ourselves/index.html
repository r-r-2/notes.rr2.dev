My talk: Labels we give ourselves, and outgrowing them

Oct 9, 2026 — Worklog • Home.

Yesterday I gave a talk at Build Hours, a special guest edition run by Open Loop Social and hosted by Roshan Abraham. It was on Zoom. I shared the session with Dima, who showed how to use AI, Anki and local LLMs to learn languages. My half was a 10-minute talk called Labels we give ourselves, and outgrowing them.

Your job title describes your past work, not your potential.

What I talked about

  1. The labels we give ourselves. “I'm not a numbers person.” “I'm not creative.” Mine was “I'm not a frontend person.”
  2. Titles are labels too. My first title was Production Engineer, the person called when things break. When I took on more development work, the title changed to Software Engineer. The title caught up with the work, not the other way round.
  3. Moments that showed me labels don't stick. In college, students from every branch got the same “IT professional” label at placement time. At work, new grads moved fast because they had no labels holding them back.
  4. What changed. I stopped deciding what I wasn't before I'd tried, and AI helped me peel the labels off faster. Since then I've built Flight Over Me, made the top 10 solo at a Bangkok hackathon, and built the Reading Room, all frontend-heavy work I was sure I couldn't do.
  5. How I learn new things. Ask AI to teach you (“explain it like I'm five”, “from first principles”), follow the best people in the field, check what's working right now, and start building before you feel ready.
  6. A 30-day challenge. Pick one label you've given yourself and build one small thing outside it. I offered free help to anyone who gets stuck on the first step.

It closed on the line the whole talk was building to: you can just do things.
