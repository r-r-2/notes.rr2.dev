TL;DR
Karpathy shipped HackerTube as a weekend hack: the YouTube videos Hacker News keeps telling each other to watch. It didn't have a launch video, so I made one with Opus. It's a 41-second video built entirely in code, using real HN comments and real data from the site.

The idea

I wanted the video to show what makes HackerTube different: the recommendations come from people, not from an algorithm guessing what keeps you scrolling.

So it opens on one real Hacker News thread from 2015. Someone recommends Josh Kaufman's The first 20 hours TED talk, and the first reply is "Yes! I came here to post this exact comment." That one reply is basically the whole idea of HackerTube.

What's in it

  • The thread, rebuilt to look like real HN.
  • A timeline of every time that talk was recommended, from 2014 to 2023, each one in a completely different thread.
  • A comparison of how other favourite talks keep getting recommended year after year.
  • 43 million comments distilled into 6,454 videos, shown as a wall of real thumbnails.
  • The site itself: each video's page lists who recommended it and where, plus categories, playlists and Longs.

How it was made

  • Everything is code. The video is HTML and CSS animated with GSAP, then rendered to MP4 with HyperFrames.
  • Every comment, date and count is real. Claude read them from HackerTube's own data through the browser, and checked a few against the original HN threads.

What I learned

  • Readability beats cleverness. The first versions had a moving timeline and comment cards that flew past too fast to read. The version that worked is still, with fewer, bigger words.
  • Small wording matters. Captions that sounded fine were sometimes wrong. "Browse by topic, not by feed" isn't true, because the site has a feed. So every line got checked against the actual site.
  • Music is still hard to do in code. Claude generated four soundtracks, from synth to marimba to keys, and none of them felt right. A real track from Pixabay, cut so its lift lands on the "6,454" moment, worked first try.
  • Iterating out loud works. Almost every good decision came from me saying "this feels weird" and working out why before building anything.
